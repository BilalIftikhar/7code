**Short answer:** SEO gets your pages to rank on Google. AEO (answer engine optimisation) gets your content picked as *the answer* — in featured snippets, voice search and AI overviews. GEO (generative engine optimisation) makes your business easy for AI assistants like ChatGPT and Gemini to understand and mention. They overlap heavily, and they all start from the same foundation: a fast site with clear, honest, well-structured pages.

## The difference between SEO and AEO

**SEO gets your page into the results. AEO gets your words used as the answer.** SEO is judged by rankings and clicks; AEO is judged by whether a search engine, voice assistant or AI overview quotes your content directly.

| | SEO | AEO |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rank your page for a search | Be the direct answer to a question |
| Where it shows | Google's results list | Featured snippets, "People also ask", voice answers, AI Overviews |
| What you change | Site speed, structure, titles, content depth, links | How answers are written: question headings, a short answer first, FAQ markup |
| Typical unit of work | A page | A question |
| How you measure it | Rankings, clicks, organic leads | Snippet and AI Overview appearances, question-query impressions |

In practice the two share most of the work. A slow or badly structured page won't rank, and a page that doesn't rank is rarely chosen as the answer. That's why we treat AEO as the way you write on top of good SEO rather than a separate project.

## Why three acronyms now

For twenty years, getting found online meant one thing: ranking in Google's blue links. That still matters. But customers now also ask questions and get an answer without clicking anything — from a featured snippet, a voice assistant, or an AI chat.

A site manager in Abu Dhabi might search "50 ton crane hire Mussafah" and call the first result. The same person might instead ask an AI assistant "who rents mobile cranes near KIZAD?" and get two or three company names back. You want to be found in both cases. That's where the three disciplines come in.

## SEO: getting your pages ranked

Search engine optimisation is the work of making your pages the best result for a search and making them easy for search engines to read.

In practice it comes down to three layers:

- **Technical:** the site loads fast, works on phones, can be crawled, has no duplicate pages competing with each other, and uses canonical tags, sitemaps and redirects correctly.
- **On-page:** each page has one clear purpose, a unique title and description, a single H1, and content that genuinely answers what the searcher wants.
- **Authority:** other reputable sites mention and link to you, and your business details are consistent across the web.

If you're a service business, SEO is still where most of your enquiries will come from. Our [SEO, AEO and GEO service](/services/seo-aeo-geo) starts here for that reason.

## AEO: becoming the answer

Answer engine optimisation is about formatting your knowledge so a search engine can lift it directly as the answer.

The core habits are simple:

- **Lead with the answer.** Put a one- or two-sentence direct answer at the top of a section, then explain. (This article does exactly that.)
- **Use real questions as headings.** Phrase headings the way customers ask: "How long does crane hire take to book?", not "Booking timelines".
- **Mark up your FAQs.** FAQ structured data tells search engines exactly which text answers which question.
- **Keep answers self-contained.** An answer pulled out of context should still make sense on its own.

AEO rarely needs new pages. Most of the time it means rewriting the pages you already have so the answers are easy to find.

## GEO: being understood by AI assistants

Generative engine optimisation is the newest of the three, and the one with the most hype. Here is what it honestly involves.

AI assistants build their answers from what they've learned and from sources they retrieve. They're more likely to mention a business when its information is **clear, consistent and corroborated**. So GEO work focuses on:

- **Entity clarity:** your company name, what you do, where you operate and how to contact you stated plainly and identically on your site, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn and directories.
- **Descriptive pages:** service and location pages that say specifically what you do and for whom, not vague marketing copy.
- **Original information:** case studies, processes and data that only you can publish. Generic content gives an AI no reason to cite you.
- **Structured data:** Organization, Service, FAQ and Article markup that removes ambiguity.

What GEO can't do is guarantee placement. Anyone promising your business will appear in ChatGPT answers is overpromising. The goal is to make your business the easiest, most trustworthy source to use.

## How the three fit together

Think of it as one stack rather than three separate projects:

1. **SEO** is the foundation. If your site is slow or confusing, nothing else works.
2. **AEO** is how you write on top of that foundation: answer-first, question-led, marked up.
3. **GEO** is how you present your business as a whole: consistent, specific, and backed by real evidence.

A page that does all three well tends to rank, gets quoted in snippets, and gives AI assistants exactly what they need to describe you accurately.

## What this looks like on a real site

On [sevenlift.net](/work/seven-lift), a crane and equipment hire company in Abu Dhabi, we combined all three: fast server-rendered pages and one page per district (SEO), answer-first FAQs with structured data (AEO), and consistent, specific business information for AI tools (GEO). Over three months Google Search Console recorded 357 organic clicks from 23,000 impressions, with daily clicks rising from a handful in August to peaks of 11 to 16 a day by late October.

That isn't a dramatic overnight number, and it shouldn't be: it's what steady, honest foundations look like in their first months, and it compounds as more pages earn their place.

## Where to start

If you're unsure where your site stands, work through this list in order:

1. Is your site fast on a mid-range phone? Check it in Google's PageSpeed Insights.
2. Does each service and each location you serve have its own genuinely different page?
3. Do your pages answer the top questions customers ask you on the phone?
4. Are your business name, address and phone number identical everywhere online?
5. Do you publish anything original — case studies, processes, real examples?

The first two are SEO. The third is AEO. The last two are GEO. Fix them in that order.
