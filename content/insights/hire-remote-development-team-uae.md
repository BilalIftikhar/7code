**Short answer:** agree a written scope and price first, keep the code and hosting on your own accounts, demand a staging link you can open any time, pay in milestones, and pick a team whose day overlaps with yours. Get those five things right and most of the risk of remote development disappears.

## Why UAE companies hire remotely

Building software in-house in Dubai or Abu Dhabi means recruiting, visas and salaries for skills you may only need for a few months. A remote team gives you senior people for the length of the project, and the best ones work in a way that's easier to supervise than an in-house hire.

The risks are real too: projects that stall halfway, code you can't access, and invoices that grow faster than the product. Almost all of them come down to how the engagement is set up, not where the team sits.

## 1. Put the scope in writing before any work starts

A good remote team will push *you* for clarity before quoting. Expect a short discovery call followed by a written proposal that lists:

- every page, feature or integration included,
- what is explicitly **not** included,
- the timeline, broken into stages,
- the price per stage.

If a team quotes a firm price after a five-minute chat, that price will move later. Clear scope protects both sides.

## 2. Own everything from day one

This is the single most important rule. Ask for:

- **Code** in a repository on *your* GitHub or GitLab account, with the team added as collaborators.
- **Hosting** (Vercel, AWS, your server) under your account and billing.
- **Domain** registered in your company's name.
- **Third-party accounts** — email delivery, payment gateways, analytics — opened by you.

If the relationship ends, you keep working software and simply remove access. A team that resists this is a team to avoid.

## 3. See progress, don't just hear about it

Weekly status messages are not progress. A **staging link** is. From the early weeks you should be able to open a working version of your site or product, click through it and leave comments.

Pair that with a shared task board, so you can see what's done, what's next and what's waiting on you.

## 4. Pay for delivered stages

Milestone payments tie money to visible results: a deposit to start, then a payment when each agreed stage is live on staging and approved. For ongoing work — SEO, maintenance or a dedicated developer — monthly billing against a clear list of priorities works better.

## 5. Choose a team whose day overlaps with yours

Time zones matter more than distance. Pakistan, for example, is just **one hour ahead of the UAE**, so a Lahore team's working day lines up almost exactly with yours. Questions get answered the same day and calls are easy to schedule.

Agree on channels up front. Many UAE businesses prefer WhatsApp for quick questions and email for decisions, with calls when something needs discussion.

## 6. Check the work, not the pitch

Before signing, look for:

- **Live projects you can open** — not just screenshots.
- **Honest case studies** that say what the team actually did, including when they contributed to part of a larger product.
- **Relevant experience.** If you run a logistics or equipment business, a team that has built for one will need less explanation.
- **A senior person who stays on the project**, not just in the sales call.

## A quick checklist

Before you sign with any remote team, make sure you can tick every line:

- [ ] Written scope, exclusions, timeline and price per stage
- [ ] Code, hosting, domain and accounts in your name
- [ ] Staging link from the early weeks
- [ ] Milestone or monthly payments tied to delivered work
- [ ] Working hours that overlap with yours
- [ ] Live, verifiable examples of past work
- [ ] A named senior lead for the whole project
